haikai: privacy policy

haikai (com.abm.haikai) is a game for young children made by abm. It does not collect names, contact details, location or any advertising identifier. It sends anonymous fault reports to abm so that crashes can be fixed, and, only if a grown-up agrees, anonymous play data. Both can be switched off, and what was sent can be deleted from inside the game.

Who is responsible

abm, based in the United Kingdom, makes haikai and decides what the play data described here is used for (in data protection law, abm is the "controller"). You can reach abm at abm.ai.studios@gmail.com.

What the game collects

The game has no accounts and no sign-in. It asks for no name, email address, age, location, photos or contacts. It does not read the advertising ID, the phone number or any hardware identifier.

The game's Settings screen has a line called Sharing with three choices.

  • Fault reports only. This is how the game starts. When the game hits a program error, or was closed unexpectedly the time before, it sends: the text of the error and where in the game it happened; the game's version, the device model, the Android version, the screen size, the graphics type and the amount of memory; and a random number made by the game when it is installed, with a second one for each time it is opened. These numbers are not taken from the device, and a reinstall makes a new one.
  • Fault reports and play data. Only after a grown-up answers "Yes, share" to the question the game asks the first time it is opened (or chooses it in Settings). The game then also sends what happened in play: which day was played and whether it was won, coins and stars earned, drinks served, customers who left, upgrades bought with in-game coins, settings changed, and how long the game was open.
  • Nothing. The game sends nothing at all.

The address the data comes from is used for a few seconds to limit how often one device may send, and is not stored.

Why, and who sees it

abm uses the data only to find and fix faults and to balance the game's difficulty. It is not used for advertising or to build a profile of anyone, and it is not sold or shared with anyone else. It is sent over an encrypted connection and kept on servers in the European Union (Amazon Web Services, Ireland), which store it for abm.

The legal ground

For fault reports abm relies on its legitimate interest in keeping the game working: they are limited to what fixing a fault needs, they do not identify a person, and they can be switched off. Play data is sent only with the agreement of a grown-up, which can be withdrawn in Settings at any time. abm does not ask for consent to advertising or profiling because it does neither. abm follows the United Kingdom's data protection law (UK GDPR). In the terms of the United States children's privacy rule (COPPA), the random install number is used only to support the internal operations of the game.

How it is protected

The data travels over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) and is stored encrypted. Only abm can read it. The server accepts play data but gives none out, so nobody can read another player's data through the game.

How long it is kept, and how to stop it

Each record is deleted automatically 90 days after it arrives. To stop sending, open Settings in the game and set Sharing to "nothing": the game then sends nothing and drops anything not yet sent. To delete what the game has already sent, open Settings, tap Privacy, then tap "Delete my play data" twice: the game asks abm's server to remove every record of this install, sets Sharing to "nothing" and takes a new random number. Records also expire by themselves after 90 days.

Ads and purchases

The game contains no advertising, no in-app purchases and no third-party software kits that collect data. The game engine's own statistics reporting is switched off. The only internet address the game talks to is abm's play-data server.

Where progress is kept

The café is saved in a small file on the device. If Android backup is switched on, Android includes that file in the device's own backup to the Google account on the device; abm cannot see it. Deleting the game deletes the save, and Settings in the game has a Reset progress button.

Children and parents

The game is designed for children and follows the Google Play Families policy. What is described above is the only information collected from anyone, including children. It contains nothing that identifies a child, and it is used only to keep the game working and balanced. The game shows no advertising and has no links that lead a child out of the game.

A parent or guardian can at any time:

  • choose what is sent: in the game, open Settings and tap Sharing until it reads "fault reports only", "fault reports and play data" or "nothing". Play data is never sent unless a grown-up has chosen it;
  • delete what was sent: in the game, open Settings, tap Privacy, then tap "Delete my play data" twice. The records of that install are removed from abm's server at once;
  • ask abm what play data it holds from a device, or ask abm to delete it for you, by writing to abm.ai.studios@gmail.com. Give us the code shown at the bottom of the game's Settings screen (the start of the install's random number), or the device model and the days on which the game was played. We answer within 30 days;
  • do nothing: every record is deleted by itself 90 days after it arrives, and removing the game stops everything.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see the data held about you or your child, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to its use, and to complain to your data protection authority (in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner's Office, ico.org.uk). Write to abm.ai.studios@gmail.com to use any of these rights; you do not need to give a reason.

Changes to this policy

If a later version of the game changes any of this (for example by adding advertising or collecting anything more), this page and the statement inside the game (Settings, Privacy) will be updated before that version is released.
